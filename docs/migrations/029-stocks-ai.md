# 029 — Computer stock purchases and sales

This module ports the original Rich4 v3.11 routines `0x42bf03` (buy) and
`0x42c79f` (sell), recovered in GPL-3.0-or-later
[mytbk/rich4](https://github.com/mytbk/rich4) `asm/rich4.asm`. It uses portable
`World`, per-game `Random`, the previously verified stock transactions, and the
integer-backed 64-significand arithmetic from migration 023. No original machine
code, Windows API, 32-bit pointer layout, or host floating-control state is used
at runtime.

`stocks_ai::buy/sell(World&, Random&, player, market_closed)` directly applies the
original transactions and returns their ordered `{stock, quantity, value,
commercial_owner_changed}` records. The game supplies its original calendar/news
closure result and displays records afterwards. Callers must not prefilter these
functions or draw additional random values: their probability gates are part of
the original behavior.

## Purchase behavior

A buy visit draws its `rand()%3` gate first, then checks the profile's investment
percentage (original player byte 26), market closure, and whether an outstanding
loan is fewer than fifteen days away. Its budget uses the exact stock-valuation
loop: each prior integer subtotal is converted to binary32 before adding the
next extended-precision holding value and truncating. The target investment
percentage uses wrapping signed-32 multiplication and truncating division.

Twelve stocks receive the original company or standalone-market scores. Company
scores use month-averaged profit, valuation per 10,000 shares, cash balance, and
whether available shares can overtake the incumbent shareholder. Standalone
scores use their baseline, signed movement-bit comparisons, and averages of the
last 24 and 6 nonzero history samples. The source's strict/non-strict comparisons
and different 20,000/30,000-times-price-index bank thresholds are preserved.
Suspension, limit-up, and zero trade quota exclude a stock.

The original calls Watcom `qsort` for twelve packed score/index entries. Its
less-than-sixteen branch first insertion-sorts positions 0,3,6,9, then performs
ordinary insertion sort over the entire array. This can reorder tied stocks.
The port reproduces both passes rather than substituting a host library sort.
For each nonzero score in sorted order it consumes `rand()%24` and accepts when
that value is at most `12-rank`. At most one stock is bought, with quantity bounded
by the exact truncated budget/quote result and the current trade quota.

## Sale behavior

A loan due within six days whose amount exceeds current liquid funds triggers an
urgent sale visit, bypassing the ordinary `rand()%3` gate. Otherwise the random
gate precedes market closure. Zero loan-date storage keeps the original day-index
value −1. Loan dates generated by packed month addition also retain overflowed
days, such as November 31 being counted as December 1.

The original company and standalone-market scores are preserved, including the
company routine's actual `FDIVRP` calculation **total shares / own shares**. This
is deliberately not rewritten into an intuitive ownership fraction. Standalone
sales retain average-price declines, profit-ratio thresholds, historical minimum,
liquidity thresholds, and urgent-score doubling. Suspended and limit-down stocks
are excluded. The highest positive score wins, with first stock ID winning ties.
Its entire holding is sold to the market and credited to the bank.

An urgent visit recomputes every score and may sell additional holdings until
liquid funds reach 1.1 times the loan, or no qualifying holding remains. These
repeat passes draw no extra random values. Company ownership and market share
counters update through the same verified `stocks::sell_stock` transaction.

`stocks::update_commercial_owner` is also exposed for the original player-to-player
stock-transfer routine. It reuses the existing shareholder reinsert algorithm and
does not change funds, holdings, or market quotas. Equal holdings retain the
other incumbent's priority.

## Numeric and input boundaries

Every original binary32 intermediate is explicit: history accumulation/averages,
company profit and integer per-share valuation, holding ratios, and thresholds.
Mixed double constants are evaluated at the original 64-significand precision;
integer conversions truncate and overflow to the x87 indefinite integer. Signed
integer addition and multiplication explicitly keep their low 32 bits.

The supported game-state domain is the existing stock module's finite normal
quotes and history values, at most four players, valid company references, and
history cursor 0..143. Zero positive cost basis is handled as an infinite sale
ratio, retaining the original indefinite-integer scoring behavior. This is not a
new policy for malformed saves containing arbitrary NaNs or invalid references.

## Independent executable comparison

`scripts/run_stocks_ai_oracle.py` loads the user's original EXE into the existing
isolated Unicorn PE harness. Original AI scores, Watcom sorting, date arithmetic,
RNG, price-limit calculations, buy/sell transactions, and shareholder updates all
execute original instructions. UI panel/text/message calls are stubbed; market
closure is a fixture input read from guest memory, and CRT thread state uses
private guest storage. The precisely disclosed FPREM correction from migration
015 remains necessary for Unicorn's price-rounding boundary defect. This is an
instruction-level oracle with listed adapters, not an unqualified hardware claim.

There are 1,024 complete visits (512 states × buy/sell), including **96 purchases,
833 sales, and 122 visits with multiple sales**. Native ASan/UBSan tests match all
of the following exactly for every visit:

* Final Watcom RNG state and ordered trade stock IDs/quantities.
* Cash and bank balance.
* All twelve holding quantities and cost-basis binary32 bits.
* All twelve market availability/quota values.
* All four linked company owners and their complete shareholder order.

This includes purchases of both linked and standalone stocks, all twelve sold
stock IDs, market closures, suspensions/caps, investment-profile gates, loan
deadlines, history cursor wrapping, and both month-zero and averaged profits.
The capture script refuses to publish a default-size run that produces no buys,
no sales, or no repeated-sale visits. This prevents an accidentally constant
closure adapter from generating a vacuous passing oracle.

```sh
.local/stocks-oracle/venv/bin/python scripts/run_stocks_ai_oracle.py
build/native/stocks_ai_tests .local/stocks-ai-oracle/latest/visits.csv
```

The private CSV and manifest record the original EXE hash, output hash, adapters,
and execution counts under `.local/stocks-ai-oracle/`. They contain no copied
game asset content and are not bundled with the native game. The standalone
unit checks additionally lock down gate/closure RNG ordering. Strict conversion
warnings and ASan/UBSan passed for this migration.
